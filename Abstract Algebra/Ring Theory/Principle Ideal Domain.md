# Principle Ideal Domain
A [[Principle Ideal Domain]] (PID) is an [[Integral Domain]], $R$, in which every ideal is generated by a single element, or putting it more mathematically, we can express some ideal as the following:
$$\langle a \rangle = \{ ra: r \in R \} \text{ for fixed } a\in R $$

---
# Theorem). 
Let $F$ be a [[Field]]. Then $F[x]$ is a PID. 

$Proof).$ 
We know that $F[x]$ is an integral domain by a previous result. If $I=\{0\} \implies I=\langle 0 \rangle$. If $I \neq \{0\}$, then let $g(x)$ be a [[Polynomial]] of minimum degree such that $g(x) \in I$. We want to show now that $I=\langle g(x) \rangle$. 

We know that $g(x) \in I \implies \langle g(x) \rangle \subseteq I$. Consider $f(x) \in I$ now. Via the division algorithm we are to express $g(x)q(x)+r(x)=f(x)$ where $0\leq\text{deg}(r(x))<\text{deg}(g(x))$. 

Since $r(x)=f(x)-q(x)g(x) \in I$, by the minimality of $g(x) \implies \text{ deg}(r(x))=0$. 

$$\begin{align}
\therefore \langle g(x) \rangle = I  \\
&& \blacksquare
\end{align}$$
### Theorem). Criterion for $I=\langle g(x) \rangle$
Let $F$ be a field. Let $I$ be an [[Ideal (Rings)]] that is non-zero in $F(x)$ and $g(x) \in F(x) \implies I=\langle g(x) \rangle  \iff g(x) \neq 0$ and $\text{deg}(g(x))$ is minimal in $I$. 

This result follows from the above proof.

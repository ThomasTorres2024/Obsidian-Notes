# The Class Equation 
For some [[Group]] $G$ we have that, given the [[Center of a Group]] and its remaining [[Conjugacy Class]]es then:
$$\large |G| = |Z(G)|+ \sum_{x_i \not \in Z(G)}|\text{cl}_{G}(x_{i})|$$

For some [[Set]] $X$ and some group $G$ acting on said set in the perspective of [[Group Action]]s we have that:
$$|X|= \left| X_{g} \right| + \sum_{i=1}^n |O_{x_{i}}| : x_{i} \text{ forms distinct orbits}$$
Recall that $|O_{x_{i}}|=[G:G_{x_{i}}]$.  

---
### Theorem). 
Let $G$ be a [[Group]] of order $p^n$ where $p$ is [[Prime]], then $Z(G) \neq \{ e\}$ ([[Center of a Group]]). 

$Proof.)$ 
By [[Lagrange's Theorem]] the following must be true for some [[Centralizer]] subgroup of element $x \in G$:
$$[G:C_{G}(x)]=\frac{|G|}{|C_{g}(x)|}= \frac{p^n}{p^k}=p^{n-k} > 1 \iff x \not \in Z(G)$$
If $x \in Z(G) \implies p^{n-k}=1$. Thus by the class equation:
$$\large \begin{align}
p^n = |G|=|Z(G)|+\sum_{x_{i}}[G:C_{G}(x_{i})] \\
=|Z(G)|+ mp \implies |Z(G)|=p^n -mp  > 1  
\end{align}$$

### Corollary
If $|G|=p^2$ then $G$ is an [[Abelian Group]]. 

$Proof.)$
By the theorem, we know that the size of $Z(G)$ is either $p$ or $p^2$. If $|Z(G)=p^2|$ then $G$ is abelian. If $Z(G)=p \implies \left|\frac{G}{Z(G)}\right|=p$. Therefore $\frac{G}{Z(G)}$ is a [[Cyclic Group]] which is generated by the coset $\langle gZ(g) \rangle$. 

If we take $x,y\in G \implies x\in g^mZ(G)$ and $y \in g^nZ(g)$ for $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$. Then we have that:
$$x=g^nz_{_{1}} \quad y=g^mz_{2} \quad  z_{1},z_{2} \in Z(G)$$
But then:
$$xy=(g^n z_{1})(g^m z_{2})=yx$$
This would contradict the original statement then. We assumed that the order of the center was $p<p^2$ but we have shown that $G$ is [[Abelian Group]] which would mean that the order of the center is $p^2$.
